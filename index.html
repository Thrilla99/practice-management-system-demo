Stop 1 / 8 · Calendar

You plan the week before you work it

Client work gets assigned onto a week that knows its own capacity. The firm's real rules are in here: 8.5 hours Monday to Thursday, 5.5 hours on a Friday, public holidays excluded. "Full" means full, and you see over-assignment before it happens, not after. Chips carry each staff member's colour, same as the production calendar.

Stop 2 / 8 · Timesheet

Hours go in as quarters, against real work

Staff log decimal time against a client and a work type: VAT201, annual financials, payroll runs. Every entry carries a billable flag, and that flag is a human decision, not an automatic one. New entries land in a queue rather than a ledger. Nothing counts until someone senior says it does.

Stop 3 / 8 · Approvals

Nothing counts until a lead has looked at it

Every entry passes through a team lead. Approve confirms it into the firm's numbers; query bounces it back with the reason attached. This loop is what makes the dashboards worth trusting, and every decision in it goes on the record (see Stop 7).

Stop 5 / 8 · Budget analysis

Budget vs actual, per client, and the flags that make money

Every client carries an hours budget imported from the firm's fee schedule (via an AI-assisted import wizard, human-confirmed). The variance engine compares pace-adjusted actuals against it and raises two flags worth real money. Fee-adjustment candidates: consistently over budget, so the fee is probably wrong. Unbudgeted work: hours landing on a client with no budget at all, which is work the firm may be doing for free. Clients also bill on their own cycles (monthly, quarterly, annual), and the pace maths respects that.

Stop 6 / 8 · SARS letters

Every SARS letter gets an owner

Letters from SARS, the tax authority, used to land in one inbox and quietly get old. Now a letter is uploaded or pasted straight from the scanner, matched to its client, and routed onto one person's Today list, where it sits owned and dated until it's dealt with. A letter that can't be closed yet gets flagged busy with a reason: waiting on SARS, waiting on the client, or submitted. Busy is an annotation, not a status, so people can show progress without pretending the work is done.

The flags age too. A busy item past its follow-up date turns red and floats to the top, and a firm-wide oversight board splits everything pending into busy vs untouched. "It's being handled" stops being something people say in the corridor and becomes something you can check.

Stop 7 / 8 · Audit log

Nothing happens off the record

111 event types flow into an append-only audit log: every entry, approval, query, edit, deletion, merge, letter and role action, all reconstructable after the fact. For an accounting firm, being able to prove control over its time records counts for nearly as much as the records themselves.

This one is live. The log below has been recording you since the page loaded: every theme change, role switch, assignment, entry, letter and approval you performed on this tour.
